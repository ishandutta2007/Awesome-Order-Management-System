# Awesome-Order-Management-System

## Top Order Management Systems (OMS) Ecosystem

**Curated List of SaaS Products & Open-Source GitHub Projects**

*Focused on Omnichannel Order Orchestration, Inventory Promising, Fulfillment Routing, Returns & Distributed Order Management*

**Last updated: August 2026**



This repository tracks notable **SaaS platforms** and **open-source projects** for **Order Management Systems (OMS)**. These systems capture orders from multiple channels, allocate inventory, route fulfillment (warehouse, store, drop-ship), manage exceptions, and handle returns — forming the operational backbone of modern retail and commerce.



**Examples** include Fluent Commerce, IBM Sterling OMS, Manhattan OMS, Brightpearl, Cin7, Extensiv OMS, Anchanto, Kibo Commerce, NewStore, and Salesforce OMS (the category leaders).



**Open-source emphasis**: Order management and commerce have a strong open-source ecosystem. **Medusa**, **Saleor**, **Vendure**, **Sylius**, and dedicated OMS projects provide robust self-hosted foundations for order lifecycle management. This section is heavily expanded with these platforms and related tools.



Contributions welcome! Open a PR to add/update entries. Keep descriptions factual and link to official sites.

## SaaS/Hosted Platforms

- **[Fluent Commerce](https://fluentcommerce.com/)**  

  Cloud-native distributed order management platform focused on flexible orchestration, BOPIS, ship-from-store, and high-volume omnichannel retail.



- **[IBM Sterling Order Management](https://www.ibm.com/products/sterling-order-management)**  

  Long-established enterprise OMS with deep configurability for complex, multi-node fulfillment networks and global retail/B2B operations.



- **[Manhattan Active Order / OMS](https://www.manh.com/)**  

  Enterprise unified commerce and order management solution tightly integrated with Manhattan’s broader supply-chain and store systems.



- **[Brightpearl](https://www.brightpearl.com/)**  

  Retail operations platform combining order management, inventory, and accounting oriented toward growing omnichannel brands.



- **[Cin7](https://www.cin7.com/)**  

  Cloud inventory and order management platform popular with multi-channel sellers needing connected stock and order workflows.



- **[Extensiv OMS](https://www.extensiv.com/)**  

  Order and warehouse management capabilities aimed at 3PLs and brands managing multi-channel fulfillment.



- **[Anchanto](https://www.anchanto.com/)**  

  Omnichannel order and inventory management platform with strong presence in Asia-Pacific markets.



- **[Kibo Commerce](https://kibocommerce.com/)**  

  Composable commerce and order management platform supporting unified customer and order experiences across channels.



- **[NewStore](https://www.newstore.com/)**  

  Omnichannel platform focused on store and digital order orchestration for modern retail brands.



- **[Salesforce Order Management](https://www.salesforce.com/)**  

  Order management capabilities within the Salesforce Commerce and Service Cloud ecosystem for connected customer journeys.



## Open-Source GitHub Projects

- **[Medusa](https://github.com/medusajs/medusa)**  

  Flexible, modular open-source commerce platform (Node.js/TypeScript) with strong order, inventory, and fulfillment primitives. Highly extensible for custom OMS-style workflows.



- **[Saleor](https://github.com/saleor/saleor)**  

  Composable, GraphQL-first open-source headless commerce platform with robust order management, multi-channel support, and production-scale deployments.



- **[Vendure](https://github.com/vendure-ecommerce/vendure)**  

  TypeScript/NestJS headless commerce framework with plugin architecture, solid order and inventory modules, and strong suitability for B2B and custom workflows.



- **[Sylius](https://github.com/Sylius/Sylius)**  

  Symfony-based open-source e-commerce framework with flexible order processing, promotions, and extensibility for tailored OMS needs.



- **[Bagisto](https://github.com/bagisto/bagisto)**  

  Laravel-based open-source e-commerce platform including order management, multi-inventory, and admin tools suitable for mid-market use.



- **[OpenOMS](https://github.com/openoms-org/openoms)**  

  Dedicated open-source Order Management System for e-commerce with multi-tenant support, marketplace integrations, automation rules, and packing workflows.



- **[Odoo Sales / Inventory](https://github.com/odoo/odoo)**  

  Full open-source ERP suite with mature sales order, inventory, and warehouse modules that can serve as an OMS for many organizations.



- **[WooCommerce order ecosystem](https://github.com/woocommerce)**  

  WordPress-based commerce with extensive order management plugins and extensions for smaller multi-channel setups.



- **[Order orchestration and rules engines](https://github.com/)**  

  Open libraries and examples for building custom allocation, routing, and exception-handling logic on top of commerce cores.



- **[Fulfillment and multi-warehouse open tools](https://github.com/)**  

  Community projects for inventory visibility, pick/pack workflows, and carrier integrations that complement core OMS functionality.



### Additional Strong Open-Source Options

- Combining Medusa/Saleor/Vendure with open WMS or 3PL connectors for end-to-end fulfillment.

- Using Odoo or ERPNext as a unified order + inventory + accounting backbone.

- Event-driven architectures (Kafka, NATS) layered on open commerce cores for high-volume orchestration.

- Open returns and reverse-logistics modules integrated with the order lifecycle.

- Headless storefronts (Next.js, etc.) paired with open OMS backends for full control.



**Frameworks for building custom systems**: Choose **Medusa**, **Saleor**, or **Vendure** as the commerce/OMS core, extend order and inventory modules with custom allocation and routing logic, and integrate marketplaces, carriers, and warehouses via plugins or APIs. For broader ERP needs, start with **Odoo**. This stack delivers complete ownership of order data and business rules with no per-order SaaS fees — ideal for brands with engineering capacity — while commercial enterprise OMS platforms still lead in extreme multi-node complexity, proven global scale, deep retail-specific fulfillment orchestration, and vendor support for large transformations.

## Disclaimer

- This is a **community-curated** list — not exhaustive and not an endorsement.

- Order management sits at the heart of revenue, inventory accuracy, and customer experience. Open-source platforms provide excellent flexibility and cost control but still require careful design for concurrency, inventory correctness, payment compliance, and operational resilience. Thorough testing, monitoring, and fallback processes are essential before production use at scale.

- Always align OMS choices with finance, operations, and customer-service requirements, and ensure proper handling of tax, returns, and regulatory obligations in each market.
